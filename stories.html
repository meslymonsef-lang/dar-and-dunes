Fes

A CITY OF LAYERS

Fes, across the centuries

Founded in the ninth century, Fes flourished under the Marinids in the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries. Much of the medina’s urban fabric and its major monuments date from that period.

For a visitor, that history invites a slower way of looking: not just at an individual doorway, but at how residences, fountains and places of learning form a whole city.

Marrakech

THE RED CITY, IN CONTEXT

Marrakech and the Almoravids

UNESCO dates Marrakech’s foundation by the Almoravids to 1070–1072. The city went on to become an important political, economic and cultural center, with influence reaching across North Africa and Andalusia.

Read the city as more than a collection of sights. Its walls, gardens and monuments tell a longer story than a quick stop can capture.

Essaouira

A WINDOW TO THE ATLANTIC

Essaouira, a meeting of horizons

Essaouira’s fortified town is a notable example of late-eighteenth-century planning. Its trading port connected Morocco and the Saharan hinterland with Europe and the wider world.

Walk toward the harbor with that connection in mind. The sea is not simply a backdrop; it is part of the city’s story.
